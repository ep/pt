01 · The shift

IP isn't what it used to be.

The cost of creating content is collapsing. A framework, a model, a curriculum: things that once took years of expertise can now be drafted in an afternoon. What can't be generated is the experience around it.

You own the content. Your facilitators are good. But delivering it the way clients now expect (interactive, AI-assisted, accessible, branded, global) is a multi-year engineering project that quietly turns into a software company.

Clients used to accept a great deck with a polling tool bolted on. Now they want a designed experience: timed activities, live capture, AI assistance, branded artifacts they take back to work. That is the new differentiator, and it does not happen on its own.

06 · The experience

The difference between cinematic and clerical.

A polling tool gives you a word cloud. Podium gives you an experience: a room with rhythm, atmosphere, and momentum.

These touches are not decoration. They are why a session at INSEAD or Harvard feels designed rather than assembled, and they are nearly impossible for an average facilitator to manufacture live. Podium builds them in.

Fuse Timer

The room paces itself

Set a countdown for any activity. A fuse shrinks on every participant's device, synced to the facilitator's screen. Participants pace themselves, and the facilitator stops playing timekeeper.

Buzz

Momentum, built in

Celebrations, nudges, and badges that fire when participants submit on time. The difference between 90 minutes herding people to submit and 90 minutes where the work just gets done and feels fun.

Atmosphere

Designed, not assembled

Themed visuals shift between activities so each section has its own atmosphere. Music, timer chimes, submission cues, with no licensing or royalty restrictions for you to manage. The difference between cinematic and clerical.

Shout Outs

The room, moving together

Names appear on screen the moment participants submit. The first three get confetti, a competition lever the facilitator can lean into or play down. A submit counter shifts color at 90%, so the facilitator knows when to move on.

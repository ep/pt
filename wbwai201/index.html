Overview

The workshop, the modules, and what participants walk out with.

Work Better with AI 201 is ExperiencePoint's applied AI workshop for teams already using AI tools and ready to put them to work in their real workflows. A 150-minute hands-on experience built around agent building, workflow redesign, and a durable framework for where AI fits in the business.

Built for partner programs Designed to slot into your leadership development programs as a turnkey addition. You or your facilitators can deliver it: the room needs someone expert at engaging a group who understands the principles of adoption, not the deepest AI expert. This hub gives you what you need to position and propose it well.

Most AI training ends with notes and good intentions. This one ends with working AI agents teams use on Monday.

Participants build working AI agents that unlock knowledge and assets their organization already owns, often making them 10x more valuable. They storyboard a real workflow, marking the friction points for agents and the judgment points for humans. Then they assess where AI is making work abundant and where their own skills and judgment uniquely matter, so the time automation frees up goes to their best work.

Assess Value Shifts

Not technical platform training. Not prompting jargon. Not specific to one tool. This is a workshop that suits top executives and individual contributors alike.
The frame underneath: replacement thinking (do what you already do, faster) and reimagination thinking (build what wasn't possible before). Most teams plateau in the first. WBWAI 201 builds the muscle for both.

Description
Work Better with AI 201 is a 150-minute hands-on workshop for teams already using AI tools who want to put them to work in their real workflows. Participants identify dormant resources to activate with AI, workflows to automate with humans in the loop, and where each person uniquely creates value that AI can't easily replace. They leave with three things they can use right away: working AI agents built for their own tasks, a redesigned workflow, and a durable framework for making AI decisions across teams.
Audience
People already using AI for personal productivity who are ready to redesign work with it. Works well for intact teams, cross-functional groups, individual contributors, or leadership groups. No technical prerequisites. Mixed-experience rooms are fine because each participant works on their own task or their team's shared workflow.
Learning and business outcomes
  • Built capability. Participants leave able to design AI into work, not just use it. The applied practice transfers as new tools and capabilities emerge.
  • Behavior change. Teams approach work differently afterward: clearer judgment about where AI fits and where humans stay.
  • Productivity gains. Working AI agents built into real recurring tasks, ready for teams to use immediately.
  • Process efficiency. Redesigned workflows that show where AI takes over and where human judgment must stay.
  • Strategic clarity. A durable framework for where each person uniquely creates value as AI capabilities expand.
  • Coordinated AI decisions. A shared vocabulary that lets teams make consistent calls about automation, governance, and where humans stay involved.
Format
150 minutes, with an optional 30-minute Reimagine extension. 20 to 200 participants, and smaller team sessions run well. Live, virtual or in-person. English. Platform-agnostic; works with whatever AI platforms your client's organization has approved.

Who it's for

The ideal client, the questions to ask, and the fit signals to listen for.

Ideal client
Mid-to-large organizations that have rolled out AI tools (Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, or similar) but aren't seeing the impact match the investment. Teams use AI for low-stakes tasks. Functions have different practices. The HR or IT lead wants something more strategic than tool fluency training and more concrete than abstract AI strategy consulting.
Discovery questions to ask
  1. How is AI being used across your team or organization today, and what's working or not working?
  2. What does the gap between AI access and AI impact look like inside your business?
  3. What would success look like 90 days after an AI training program for your people?
  4. Where in your organization are you most worried about how AI is shifting value or expectations?
  5. What's one workflow where you wish AI was doing more, but it isn't yet?
Not a fit if
  • The team has never used AI tools and is starting from scratch (ask us about our WBWAI 101 workshop for this need)
  • The buyer is looking for technical AI training (machine learning, model fine-tuning, building AI from scratch)
  • The buyer is looking for AI strategy consulting rather than capability building inside their own team (though when an AI strategy exists and needs to land with people, ask us about KickstartChange for the communication side of the change)

Positioning

Matching client context to positioning: the frame to use, what to recommend, and the proof to bring.

Choose the client context

Three common situations. Pick the one that best matches your client, and the pitch, recommendation, and proof below change to fit.

Leadership development program
When a leadership program needs a real AI moment
Situation

The client runs a leadership or high-potential program and knows AI has to be part of it. But a tools demo or a keynote won't land. They want leaders engaging with AI in a way that's practical and tied to real work.

They're asking: How do we make AI real for our leaders, not just another talk? How do we help them lead their teams into an AI way of working? How do we add AI without blowing up the agenda?

Positioning

Leadership programs build judgment, and AI is now where that judgment gets tested: what to automate, what to keep human, where a leader still creates the value AI can't.

The hard part is lived experience. Most leaders have talked about AI far more than they have built with it, so their guidance stays abstract.

WBWAI 201 gives every leader that experience in one 150-minute sitting. They build, they redesign real work, and they go back able to model an AI way of working for their teams instead of describing one.

Recommendation
WBWAI 201 as the applied AI module

Slot it in as the AI session inside a broader leadership journey. Leaders come away with a working agent, a redesigned workflow, and their own defensible-value statement, concrete artifacts that make the rest of your program's content land harder. Your facilitators can deliver it after a short certification, or bring in ExperiencePoint's.

In practice

Extending a client's own 101. The client had designed and delivered their own AI 101. WBWAI 201 took the same audience from general readiness to real use cases and process improvements, adapted to run even for a team without agent access.

Year three of an 'AI for everyone' journey. A program aimed at leaders ready to act, built to lower the effort of learning AI and make it obvious where it improves daily workflows. Feedback was strong enough to unlock funding for roughly 50 sessions.

Sales FAQ

Sharp answers to the questions partners hear most.

How is this different from free platform training (Microsoft Copilot, Google AI, Anthropic)?
Platform training teaches teams how to use one platform's features. It assumes that if people learn the tools, they'll figure out the impact. They mostly don't. WBWAI 201 starts with a real workflow teams own and ends with working AI agents built into it. It's also platform-agnostic, so it works regardless of which AI stack the client has licensed.
How is this different from technical AI training (machine learning, AI bootcamps)?
Technical AI training is about how AI works under the hood: models, code, datasets. WBWAI 201 is about how AI changes the business. Teams build working agents in the room using the AI tools their organization already has, but the durable value is what they leave with: a clear-eyed framework for which work AI takes over safely, which work needs human judgment, and where each role creates value AI can't easily replicate. That framework outlasts any specific platform or technique.
How is this different from what AI strategy consultants do?
Consultants tell teams what to do. WBWAI 201 builds the muscle in teams to make those decisions themselves. By the end, they've built working AI agents, redesigned a workflow, and articulated where their roles uniquely add value. The artifacts and the vocabulary stay with them. The consultant's deck doesn't.
What happens after the workshop?
Participants leave with three artifacts they own, the participant toolkit to carry them, and a vocabulary teams can share. The working agents stay in use. The redesigned workflow is theirs to expand. And the durable frames from the session, the hopes and fears they surfaced, the dormant resource map, the Value Shifts assessment, are ones their teams can rerun as AI capabilities grow. They have a clear plan for what to build next, without us.
Can our facilitators deliver it?
Yes, and you are likely better set up for it than you think. What the room needs from a facilitator is skill at engaging a group and a grounding in the principles of adoption, which is the craft you already have. The AI depth is carried for you: a self-guided agent builder walks every participant through the technical steps, and "ask AI first" is taught as a habit in the room, so it is also the graceful answer to any question nobody can field. Certification is short, and ExperiencePoint can provide facilitators where you'd rather not.
What about data security and using real company data?
Participants build agents inside the AI platforms the client's organization has already approved. They use their own work, their own files, their own context. ExperiencePoint doesn't ingest or retain that material. The workshop runs in the client's environment, within their governance.
Who should attend, and what's the right group composition?
Anyone already using AI for personal productivity who wants to move from using it to designing with it. Works well for intact teams aligning on a shared workflow, cross-functional groups, individual contributors building agents for their own tasks, or leadership groups thinking about portfolio strategy. Mixed-experience rooms work well because each person brings their own work into the room.

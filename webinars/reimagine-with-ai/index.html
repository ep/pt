Reimagine with AI

What 49 webinar attendees told us about redesigning work with AI.

What we learned

73%

73% say redesigning work with AI is a priority for the next year.

Only 7 of 49 say the focus is still rolling out tools. That's a lot of ambition. So far 35% have redesigned a workflow, so most of this group wants to go further than they have.

90%

Nearly everyone uses AI alone. Far fewer use it together.

Every step that involves other people drops off: 41% have built something others use, 35% have redesigned a workflow, and 29% have changed a role, process or hiring plan. Helping teams use AI together looks like the biggest need.

4×

Redesign happens where there's budget and owners.

Where redesign has budget and owners, 59% have redesigned a workflow. Where it's only been stated, 2 of 14 have, about the same as where it isn't a priority at all.

41%

Time is the biggest barrier, with or without a budget.

41% say people don't have time to rethink how they work. It's the top answer where redesign is funded (45%) and where it isn't (37%).

53%

Clear direction from leaders goes with getting more done.

Only 1 in 3 describe leadership-led adoption. Where leaders encourage AI but teams figure it out, results look a lot like people working on their own.

5 of 7

After time, the next barrier depends on funding.

Time tops the list either way. Without funding, no shared direction comes next: 7 of the 9 who picked it aren't funded. With funding, data comes up: 5 of the 7 who said their tools can't reach the data they need have budget and owners.

Questions this raises

  • If time is the top barrier even where there's money, is anyone budgeting for time?
  • What turns a stated priority into a funded one: a named owner, protected hours, or a budget line?
  • Six teams have built an agent others use without redesigning a workflow. Are agents being added to old processes?
  • When leaders encourage AI but leave teams to figure it out, what would useful direction look like?
  • Where is the time AI saves actually going?

About this data

  • 49 people from different organizations, each describing their own workplace.
  • They chose to attend a webinar on this topic, so they're likely further along than most.
  • The numbers are small. One person is about 2 points, and industry groups have 3 to 9 people, so treat small gaps as noise.

How this group compares

Ahead of the wider world on almost every measure, and stuck in the same place: turning personal use into team change.

35%

A step ahead on redesign.

35% of this group have redesigned a workflow with AI. McKinsey puts it at about 1 in 4 organizations, outside the top performers. 41% have built an agent or automation others use, against 30% in BCG's worker survey who say agents are already built into workflows.

90%

Much heavier AI users than the average worker.

90% use AI for their own tasks. Across US employees, Gallup finds 52% use AI in their role at all, and 30% use it a few times a week or more. This group chose to spend an hour on AI, so being ahead fits.

1 in 3

More direction from leaders than most, but still a minority.

35% say AI adoption is leadership-led, with a clear direction. Gallup finds 25% of US employees say their organization has shared a clear plan for AI.

29%

The same gap as everyone else: personal gains, little team change.

90% of this group use AI on their own, but 29% have changed a role, process or hiring plan. Gallup sees the same pattern: 65% of US employees say AI has made them more productive, but only about 1 in 10 in organizations using AI strongly agree it has transformed how work gets done.

How industries compare

Each dot is one person. Groups are small, so read these as early signals.

Tech and information is furthest along.

All 6 have redesigned a workflow and built an agent or automation that others use.

Financial services is measuring, and stretched.

All 7 call redesign a priority and 5 track business outcomes. 5 of 7 say time is what's holding them back.

Government is willing but not resourced.

6 of 8 call it a priority, but only 2 have funded it, and none have built an agent others use.

Healthcare is mostly bottom-up.

Half say people are on their own with AI, and only 1 of 6 describes leadership-led adoption.

Consultants and coaches have funding, but little redesign yet.

4 of 9 have budget and owners, about average, but only 1 has redesigned a workflow, and 5 have no formal metrics.

Business schools are just starting.

None of the 4 have funded redesign or redesigned a workflow yet.

Who came

49 people, and every one of them answered all six questions.

  • 31 from the US, 4 from Canada, 3 from Europe, and 11 didn't say.
  • Nearly 1 in 5 were consultants or coaches, the biggest single group.

What they wanted next

86%

asked for the recording and the Reimagine tool.

37%

wanted to talk about a workshop for their team or a consult.

2×

People working on their own asked for help twice as often.

Half of those who said people are mostly on their own with AI asked for a workshop or consult, against 1 in 4 in leadership-led organizations.

Who wants to go further

18 people asked to talk about a workshop for their team or a consult. Here they are, strongest interest first.

What stands out about them

6 of 10

Most who asked about a workshop already have budget and owners.

That compares with 45% of everyone. All 3 who asked about both a workshop and a consult have budget and owners.

56%

They're stuck more than they're busy.

10 of the 18 say what holds them back is no shared direction, or usage stuck on low-stakes tasks. Only 6 of the other 31 said the same. 5 of the 7 who said usage is stuck asked to talk, and so did 5 of the 9 with no shared direction.

4 of 6

Most of the strongest interest comes from government and healthcare.

They make up 4 of the 6 in the top two groups above. Half of government (4 of 8) and half of healthcare (3 of 6) asked to talk. Consulting (2 of 9), tech (1 of 6) and retail (0 of 3) asked least.

14

14 more have budget and owners but only asked for the recording.

They're further along than those who asked: 9 have redesigned a workflow and 10 have built something others use. They have money and momentum, so they're worth a conversation too.

Where they could use help

  • Shared direction. Giving teams a clear plan to follow. 14 of the 18 work where adoption isn't leadership-led: leaders encourage it and leave teams to figure it out, or people are on their own.
  • Getting past low-stakes use. Moving from quick personal wins to work that matters. 5 of the 18 say usage is stuck on low-stakes tasks.
  • Redesigning work as a team. Only 5 of the 18 have redesigned a workflow so far.
  • A way to measure. 6 of the 18 have no formal way to measure AI yet.
